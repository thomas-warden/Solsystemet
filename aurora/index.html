READING THIS MODEL

A field, not a shell.

The faint envelope represents the magnetopause, the boundary between Earth’s magnetic environment and the solar wind. It is not a solid barrier or Earth’s atmosphere. The sparse interior lines are illustrative magnetic-field guides.

The pressure control uses the approximate pressure-balance relationship R ∝ P−1/6, with 10 Earth radii at 2 nPa as an illustrative reference. The magnetic field and solar-wind field orientation are held fixed. Real boundary position also depends on those factors and changing conditions. The displayed range is 0.5–32 nPa.

Earth is enlarged relative to the magnetosphere so it remains visible. Tail length and distances are compressed; the real magnetotail extends far beyond the scene. The envelope shape and guide curves are authored illustrations, not a magnetohydrodynamic simulation.

The release sequence is separate from the pressure control. It begins with energy already stored in the magnetotail and illustrates an Earthward release and enhanced auroral emission. It does not predict a storm or imply that pressure alone determines auroral activity. Tracers indicate energy transfer, not literal packets travelling on these exact paths.

Auroras form around the magnetic poles when energetic particles excite atmospheric gases. The ovals and curtain heights are exaggerated; the magnetic axis and auroral shape are illustrative, not current geomagnetic data. Both hemispheres are shown; actual northern and southern auroras are not perfectly symmetrical. Earth and the Sun’s direction stay fixed during the sequence.
