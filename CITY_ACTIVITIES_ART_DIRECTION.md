# City activities — direction

One physical process per city, opened below the real satellite map. Use the existing serif headings, charcoal space, restrained gold controls and cool water palette. References: the app’s rendered Earth and tide exhibits and the supplied city screenshots. The tide coast’s textured terrain and raking light are the material reference; the city wheel’s quiet controls are the interaction reference.

First finish Wellington wind and Cape Town cloud. Their subjects are authored geographic illustrations: a strait between ridged islands and a flat-topped, layered mountain. They are not measured terrain or live conditions. Use textured Three.js terrain, shallow reflective water, atmospheric particles, directional light and restrained projected labels. On phones the scene remains central with controls underneath. Never use the live weather report as an input to an ideal experiment.

Each activity has a named physical variable, a visible response, a concise reading and an observation earned by deliberate input. Opening and ambient animation do not complete an activity. Reset, Pause/Play, keyboard input, live reduced-motion changes, offscreen suspension and teardown on city change are required. Only the open activity owns a rendering context. Models are explanatory relationships, not forecasts.

Extend with subject-specific scenes: layered water, delta channels, urban heat, valley drainage, a fault cutaway, peat cross-section, ice rebound, rock erosion, volcanic conduit, mangrove roots and seasonal daylight. Reuse materials and lifecycle, not identical content. All assets are original procedural geometry or existing credited exhibit textures; source and model limitations live in the footer-linked sources page.
